LEGEND: how to write a line in a data file (e.g. data/london/main.txt)
==========================================

1. A LINE IS A BOARD
   Write all the moves from the start, in SAN, separated by spaces:

       d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6

   That draws the board after 2...Nf6. Its parent is the longest shorter line it
   starts with ("d4 d5 Bf4"). The last move is highlighted in green when you
   hover over the board, and you never have to write it down.

   Move numbers are optional, and checked. "3." is for White, "3..." for Black, with
   or without a space before the move:

       1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6        1.d4 d5 2.Bf4 Nf6

   A wrong number is an error (expected "2." before Bf4), shown on the page with
   the line and the culprit marked. After a ~ @fen position the numbers are
   not checked, as the game does not start from move 1.

   Move annotations go right after a move, as in PGN: ! (good) ? (mistake)
   !! (brilliant) ?? (blunder) !? (interesting) ?! (dubious). The one on the last move
   of a line shows in the caption of its board:

       1. d4 d5 2. Bf4! c5?!

   ! and !! also get a round badge on the board, in the corner of the square the move
   went to. $7 (the PGN code for a forced move) adds a grey arrow badge, and shows as
   a square in the caption:

       1. d4 d5 2. Bf4! c5 3. e3 cxd4$7

2. BRANCHES
   Repeat the same moves and play something else:

       d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6
       d4 d5 Bf4 c5

   Two boards side by side, both under "d4 d5 Bf4".

3. COMMENTS
   Put text in {curly braces} AFTER THE LAST MOVE of the line:

       d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6 {the main line}

   A comment anywhere else in the line is ignored.

   A comment can run over several lines; it is shown beside the board as one
   left-aligned block of text (line breaks are just spaces):

       d4 d5 Bf4 {The bishop comes out before e3.
       Keep c3 for later.}

   Inside a comment: **bold**, *italic*, <br> (a forced line break) and
   [a link](https://example.com) work. A link to another page of this site
   can be relative: [Jobava](jobava.html).

   "@empty" adds a faded, empty board below a line, to suggest that the line goes
   on (only when no reply to that line is listed). Write it on its own line, under
   the line it belongs to, or at the end of that line:

       d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6 e3
       @empty

       d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6 e3 @empty

   Marks ([%cal ...], [%csl ...]) can go on
   any line and are not shown as text.
   A knight arrow can be bent in an L: add L (the long leg first) or l (the short
   leg first) after the squares, e.g. [%cal Gg1f3L,Gb1c3l]. Only for knight moves.
   [%sqr Sb1,Eb2] highlights a start (S) and an end (E) square like a last move,
   handy for a ~ @fen board that has no move of its own.
   [%sqr Rg8] marks a square red, for a check.
   Boards at the left end of a row show it on the left, at the right end on the
   right, and on a narrow screen under the board.

3b. ALTERNATIVE LINES (a board on the side, not a branch)
   A line starting with ~ is drawn as a smaller board to the LEFT of the board on
   the line right above it. It does not start a branch and has no replies:

       d4 d5 Bf4
       ~ d4 d5 Nf3 {Traditional line
       Bf4 is the accelerated version, today considered the standard.}
       d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6

   A ~ line can also start from any position, written as a FEN (just the first field, the pieces):

       ~ @fen rnbqkbnr/pppppppp/8/8/3P1B2/2P1P3/PP3PPP/RN1QKBNR {What if...}

   (White to move; then optional moves played from there, then the comment).
   With no moves there is no last-move highlight and no caption.

   The ~ line has full moves from the start, like any line. Its comment stands to
   the left of its own board on wide screens (under it on narrower ones), and its marks show on hover. Several ~ lines in a row
   stack as several side boards. The arrow keys skip them.
   On a phone they stay on the left too, just smaller (pinch to zoom).

4. MARKS (Lichess style), shown only while hovering
   Put them inside the same {comment}. The text around them is still shown; the
   codes themselves are not.

   ARROWS   [%cal Ge2e4,Rb1c3]        one arrow per item: colour + from + to
   CIRCLES  [%csl Gd4,Rf5]            one circle per item: colour + square

   Colours:  G green   R red   Y yellow   B blue
   Squares:  file a-h + rank 1-8, lowercase, e.g. e4
   Items are separated by commas (spaces work too).
   An arrow from a square to the same square is drawn as a circle.

   Habit I suggest:  R = a threat against me,  G = what I defend / my plan,
                     Y = a square to watch,    B = free to use.

5. HOME PAGE PREVIEW
   The board on the home page is set by one extra line in the opening's file:

       @preview d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6 e3 e6 c3 c5 Nd2 Nc6 Ngf3 Bd6 Bg3 O-O Bd3 {[%cal Rd3h7,Gf3e5]}

   It takes the same moves and marks as a normal line, but it does not make a
   board on the opening's own page. The marks show while hovering the card. Without it the home page follows the first line of
   each position (up to 8 moves).

6. EXAMPLES

   d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6 e3
   d4 d5 Bf4 Nf6 e3 {the bishop eyes b8 [%cal Gf4b8] [%csl Rb8]}
   d4 d5 Bf4 c5 {[%cal Rc5d4] hits d4: answer with e3 or c3}

7. ON THE PAGE
   - Down or Right arrow: next board. Up or Left arrow: previous board. The order
     is the tree's: a board, its first branch all the way down, then the next one.
   - Cmd-click (Ctrl-click on Windows/Linux) on a board: opens that position on
     the chess.com analysis board in a new tab.
   - Hover a board: the last move turns green, plus any marks you wrote.

8. THINGS TO REMEMBER
   - Lines starting with # are ignored. Blank lines too.
   - Moves must be legal in the position. If not, the page shows
     "line N: ... is not a legal move here".
   - A wrong mark shows "line N: ... is not a valid cal mark" (or csl).
   - Use x for captures (cxd4), O-O for castling, = for promotion (e8=Q).
   - Upper case for pieces (Bf4, Nf6), lower case for pawns (d4).
